# Code of Conduct

## What we are aiming for

We are a student team building something real and learning while we do it. That only works if everyone can contribute without being talked down to, ignored or harassed, whatever their background, ability, gender, race, language, religion or sexuality, and regardless of how much they already know.

The short version: asking a basic question here should feel safe.

## What we expect

* Criticise the work, not the person. "This will break when a store has no sales" is useful. "This is rubbish" is not.
* Assume everyone is capable and acting in good faith. We are all learning something we did not know last month.
* Answer questions properly. "Google it" is not an answer. If you know, explain it, or point to where the answer is.
* Give credit honestly. Name the people whose work you built on.
* Be reliable. If you cannot finish something you claimed, say so early on the issue. A missed deadline nobody warned about hurts all ten of us.
* Take feedback well. A review is about the work improving, not about you being wrong.

## What is not acceptable

* Harassment, intimidation or discrimination of any kind, anywhere we work.
* Sexual language or imagery, and unwanted attention.
* Personal attacks, insults or belittling comments.
* Sharing someone's private information without their permission.
* Deliberately damaging someone else's work, or passing it off as your own.
* Constantly disrupting discussions, reviews or meetings.

## Academic integrity

This project is assessed, so two more rules carry the same weight as everything above.

**Submit only our own work.** VUT treats plagiarism as a serious contravention of university rules and it can lead to expulsion. The brief also warns that if two groups submit the same project, every student in both groups receives zero, with no discussion about who did it first. We can explain our approach to students in other groups, but we do not give them our work.

**Reference everything we did not write.** Third party code, datasets, papers, tutorials and any AI generated material must be attributed in the project documentation.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the group operates: this repository, issues, pull requests, commit messages, the group chat, meetings and the presentation.

## Reporting a problem

If something here is not being followed, raise it with the project lead directly. If the project lead is the problem, or the matter is serious, take it to the subject lecturer.

Anything reported will be taken seriously, handled quietly, and will not be held against the person who raised it.

## Consequences

Depending on what happened, the response may be a private conversation, a public correction, losing write access to the repository, or being removed from the group. Where academic integrity is involved, it goes to the lecturer.
